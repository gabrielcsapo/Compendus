Cover behavior

Make it feel worth picking up.

Three ways a cover can respond without turning the library into a wall of buttons. Every option keeps the actual artwork intact.

How to compare
Move across the ebook, audiobook, and comic in each row. Look for weight, page depth, and whether the motion makes the format clearer.

Option A · Universal

Quiet shelf lift

The whole volume rises and turns toward you. Calm, legible, and consistent across every format—the safest extension of what is shipped now.

EbookLift + turn

Feels like selecting a volume from a table.

AudiobookLift + turn

Consistent, although it says little about sound.

ComicLift + turn

Polished and quiet enough for dense shelves.

Option B · Tactile

Crack the cover Recommended

The front cover opens a few degrees while the page block holds still. This is the most book-like gesture and stays restrained enough for the Compendus reading-room direction.

EbookCover opens 13°

A direct invitation to read; page depth becomes obvious.

AudiobookCover opens 13°

Still feels like a story object, though less audio-specific.

ComicCover opens 13°

Feels like thumbing open an issue at the shop.

Option C · Expressive

Let the format answer

Each object reveals its nature: a book pulls open, a compact disc peeks from behind the audiobook cover, and a comic splits into offset color panels.

EbookPull from shelf

A firmer perspective turn emphasizes the spine.

AudiobookCD peeks out

The disc stays underneath and reveals only a reflective crescent.

ComicPanel offset

Energetic and unmistakably comic without covering the art.
